\subsection{Rule-Selected Sentence Replacement}
\label{sec:cue-swap}

We complement deletion with a sentence-replacement diagnostic.
Let $C$ denote the rule-selected sentence and $R$ the remaining document.
Holding the recipient's $R$ fixed, we compare the original input,
deletion of $C$, replacement by a same-label donor sentence, and
replacement by a different-label donor sentence.
Donor labels refer to the source case's benchmark label, not an expert
judgment about the legal meaning of the sentence.

The protocol was fixed before test inference.
Donors were matched by selected-sentence token length
(ratio 0.8--1.25) and normalized source position
(distance at most 0.25), with self-donors and duplicate documents excluded.
We used ten paired donor draws per recipient--target pair and retained
only pairs whose complete inputs fitted within 512 tokens for both
encoder families, without redrawing donors after the visibility check.
The common eligible sets contained 68 fine, 215 imprisonment-with-labor,
and five imprisonment-without-labor recipients.
These sets are distinct from the 132-case expert-validated subset and
do not use its annotations.

We evaluated the original frozen KLUE audit checkpoint, independently
retrained KLUE checkpoints with seeds 13, 42, and 100, and KoELECTRA seed 42.
Behavioral rates were averaged over donor draws within each recipient.
Performance was computed separately for each draw using the full
5/6/5 label spaces and then averaged.
Paired bootstrap resampled recipients, not individual donor draws.
New donor-label conversion excluded draws whose original prediction
already matched the different-label donor's label; this conditional
metric was reported by checkpoint rather than averaged across seeds.

Table~\ref{tab:cue-swap-imprisonment} shows greater imprisonment
prediction sensitivity to different-label than same-label replacement.
Across the three retrained KLUE seeds, the target-flip difference was
3.66 percentage points (95\% CI: 1.61--6.14; seed SD: 0.74).
Positive target-flip contrasts were also observed for the original
audit checkpoint and KoELECTRA.

This sensitivity does not establish consistent donor-label following.
The retrained three-seed mean difference in probability assigned to the
different-label donor's label was 0.332 percentage points
(95\% CI: $-0.047$--0.876).
The fine target-flip contrast was less conclusive, and the five-case
imprisonment-without-labor analysis was exploratory.
Thus, sentence replacement supports sensitivity to rule-selected
sentence content, but provides limited evidence of directional transfer
to the donor label.

These are synthetic input interventions, not legally validated
counterfactuals. The rule-selected sentences are not assumed to be
expert-validated leakage. Intervals are conditional on the fixed donor
draws, do not fully account for shared-donor dependence, and are nominal
without multiplicity correction.
